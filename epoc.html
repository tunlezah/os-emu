EPOC32 on the Psion Series 5

Psion PLC · 16 June 1997 · the palmtop that was actually a computer

On the desk is a Psion Series 5, shut. It is not off — it is never off. It is a complete 32-bit computer in a pencil case: a pre-emptive multitasking operating system, a real filesystem, a word processor, spreadsheet, database and diary in ROM, a programming language in the box, and a keyboard a touch-typist can use — running for a day and a half on two AA cells from the newsagent. It needs no PC, no account and no network.

Open the lid and watch the keyboard slide forward to balance the screen. Then press Word on the keyboard deck: you will be writing in under a second, in the document that was open last time — it was never saved because it was never not saved. Close the lid mid-word. Open it. The cursor will be exactly where it was.

Menu — F1, or the silk-screen Menu icon Ctrl+letter — every menu item has one App buttons — the etched row; or F2–F9 Ctrl+E — close the current program Silk-screen — painted icons left of the glass Fn (Alt) + arrows — Home, End, Page up/down
