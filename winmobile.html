A real computer, in a pocket, operated with a pointer

Windows Mobile 6.1 Professional · April 2008 · on an HTC Touch Pro · August 2008

In the summer of 2008 this was the most capable phone a business traveller could carry. It multitasked properly, it had a filesystem you could browse, it edited Word and Excel documents, it did Exchange push mail with remote wipe, it ran Remote Desktop to a Windows server, it installed software from anywhere with no store and no gatekeeper, and it had a registry you could edit. It did more than the device that arrived to replace it — and you had to touch it with a stick.

Windows Mobile 6.1 Professional could, in July 2008

  • +Run several programs at once, properly, in the background
  • +Browse its own filesystem, and a storage card, with cut, copy and paste
  • +Open, edit and save Word, Excel and PowerPoint documents
  • +Push Exchange mail, calendar and contacts over the air, with policy and remote wipe
  • +Install any .cab from any source — forum, e-mail, Bluetooth, cable
  • +Copy and paste text anywhere; send and receive MMS
  • +Remote Desktop to a Windows server
  • +Recognise cursive handwriting, well, with Transcriber
  • +Turn-by-turn GPS, Wi-Fi, 3.5G, tethering, a 3.2 MP autofocus camera, video
  • +Be reflashed with an operating system a stranger built on a forum
  • +Have its registry edited, by you, to change how the shell behaved
  • +A VGA screen — 640 × 480 on 2.8 inches, twice the linear resolution of the competition
  • +A five-row hardware keyboard that slid out sideways

The device that replaced it could not, in July 2008

  • −Run third-party software in the background at all
  • −Show you a file, ever
  • −Edit a document
  • −Copy or paste a single character
  • −Send a picture message
  • −Install anything except through one company's storefront
  • −Record video, or focus its camera
  • −Offer a keyboard you could feel
  • −Be modified by its owner in any way
  •  
  •  It could be operated with a finger.
  •  Every target on it was made for one.
  •  That was the whole game, again.
The exhibit's argument: Microsoft shrank the wrong thing. Everything on this list was delivered, competently, for eight years — and the interaction model that made Windows successful on a desk made it exhausting in a hand. HTC saw it first and built TouchFLO 3D over the top, which was lovely, and which you fall straight through the moment you tap one setting too deep. Switch the rail to Finger and try to tick a checkbox. Press X on four programs and find all four still running. Then watch the memory fill.
Everything is drawn in code. No images, no fonts, no network. Your device state is kept in this browser.
